# Small-volume spectrum of four-dimensional SU(2) Yang–Mills theory

[Paper (PDF)](paper.pdf) · [LaTeX source](paper.tex) · [Verification scripts](checks/)

## 1. From continuum existence to a quantitative spectrum

OpenAI's Lean construction takes the full ultraviolet limit of separated local-field correlations at each fixed admissible Euclidean four-torus. Volume-uniform bounds then give a subsequence of any prescribed strictly increasing volume sequence along which the correlations converge on $\mathbb{R}^4$. The commutative $C^{\ast}$-algebra of observables compressed to the zero-energy space gives a phase decomposition. Each phase reconstructs a Wightman theory with a unique vacuum. Uniqueness of the infinite-volume limit and positivity of its mass gap remain open. [Lean source](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/tree/3456e5e) · [Expository construction, Sections 5–6](https://mgubi.org/yang-mills-informalization/manuscript/main.html)

The paper uses the fixed-torus Wilson coupling trajectories, blocking estimates, and local-field convergence from that construction. Theorem 1 extracts the first gap from the original Wilson transfer spectrum after removal of the cutoff. Proposition 2 identifies the same gap and multiplicity in the continuous-time vacuum representation generated by bounded, center-invariant Wilson observables at fixed nonzero physical resolution. The cyclic subspace of the specified separated renormalized local fields inherits the strict spectral lower bound. Equality on that subspace would require positive spectral weight in the first excited cluster. [Theorem 1 and Proposition 2](paper.pdf)

## 2. A forty-year-old prediction becomes a continuum theorem

Martin Lüscher developed the $g^{2/3}$ expansion for a small periodic box in 1983. Jeffrey Koller and Pierre van Baal developed the effective Hamiltonian of the spatially constant modes and studied low-lying levels and electric-flux energies in 1986–1987. Van Baal reviewed this program in 2000. The present paper gives, to our knowledge, the first quantitative derivation of the predicted first gap from the original Wilson transfer spectrum through the full ultraviolet limit along the selected Wilson trajectories. [Lüscher (1983)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(83)90436-4) · [Koller and van Baal (1986)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/055032138690252X) · [Koller and van Baal (1987)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.58.2511) · [Van Baal's review](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0008206)

The spatial box is a periodic three-torus of side $L_0$. The theorem concerns the sector with zero magnetic twist, zero electric flux, and exact zero total momentum. At fixed $L_0$ and fixed inverse-coupling parameters $b,v$, it sends the lattice spacing $a_N$ to zero along the full chosen cutoff sequence. It then sends the reference inverse coupling $b$ to infinity, so the Wilson coupling $g=(4/v)^{1/2}$ at the terminal scale tends to zero. The limiting first gap $m^{00}(L_0,v)$ satisfies

$$
L_0 m^{00}(L_0,v)
=\frac{\nu_1-\nu_0}{2}\,g^{2/3}\bigl[1+O(b^{-1/8})\bigr].
$$

Here $\nu_0<\nu_1$ are the first two distinct eigenvalues of the nine-dimensional quartic Schrödinger operator $-\Delta_X+\sum_{i<j}|X_i\times X_j|^2$ on states invariant under simultaneous rotations of the three color vectors $X_i\in\mathbb{R}^3$. The relative error is uniform over the real terminal band $|v-b|\le c_0 b^{31/32}$, where $0<c_0<1/64$, and the weak-coupling estimate holds on a common fine-cutoff tail for the whole band. [Theorem 1 and Appendix A](paper.pdf)

## 3. A rigorous weak-coupling starting point for the mass-gap problem

The quartic magnetic potential comes from the non-Abelian commutator of the spatial zero modes. Transverse quantum zero-point energy confines motion along the directions where that potential vanishes. In compact Maxwell theory on the same box and in the corresponding zero-flux, zero-momentum sector, the first excitation is a pair of photons of opposite momenta with total energy $4\pi/L_0$. That energy scale comes from momentum quantization. [Sections 1 and 6](paper.pdf)

Jaffe and Witten's Millennium Prize problem asks for existence and a positive mass gap for Yang–Mills theory on $\mathbb{R}^4$. The fixed-volume theorem gives a quantitative starting point for continuum spectral analysis at weak coupling. A proof through increasing boxes still needs control through intermediate coupling and a positive lower bound that survives as the spatial volume grows. [Official problem description](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/yangmills.pdf)
